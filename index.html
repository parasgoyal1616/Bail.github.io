BioArtificial Immune Liver (BAIL)

A Smart Implantable Platform for Real-Time Hepatitis B Detection, Immune Activation, and Liver Regeneration
Presenters: Paras Goyal, Vansh Kamboj
B. Pharmacy 2nd Year

🔬 Project Abstract

Chronic Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection remains a major global health challenge, affecting approximately 296 million people worldwide and standing as a leading cause of liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Although current antiviral therapies effectively suppress viral replication, they fail to eradicate covalently closed circular DNA (cccDNA), resulting in persistent infection, immune exhaustion, lifelong treatment, and disease recurrence. To address these limitations, we propose the BioArtificial Immune Liver (BAIL) — a conceptual smart implantable bioengineered platform that integrates regenerative medicine, immunotherapy, biosensing, and intelligent therapeutic control into a unified system.

BAIL is designed as a three-layer architecture comprising a Living Hepatic Tissue Module for continuous HBV biomarker detection and liver support, an Immune Surveillance and Activation Module incorporating engineered Kupffer cells, dendritic cells, natural killer cells, macrophages, and virus-specific cytotoxic T cells for targeted antiviral immunity, and a Smart Therapeutic Control Module integrating biosensors, AI-assisted decision-making, and microfluidic delivery for localized therapeutic intervention.

Unlike existing therapies, BAIL aims to combine real-time monitoring, localized immune modulation, continuous liver regeneration, and future integration with CRISPR-based cccDNA targeting to facilitate a functional cure — while minimizing systemic toxicity and reducing disease recurrence.

📐 BAIL Architecture: Three-Layer Smart System

Layer 1: Living Hepatic Tissue

Human iPSC-derived hepatocytes cultured within a biocompatible 3D scaffold to recreate the native liver microenvironment. Supported by endothelial cells and extracellular matrix components, this tissue continuously monitors HBV-specific biomarkers while maintaining essential hepatic functions — enabling real-time detection of infected hepatocytes and a regenerative foundation for personalized intervention.

Layer 2: Immune Surveillance

An engineered immune microenvironment for targeted viral elimination. Kupffer cells detect infected hepatocytes and present antigens; NK cells, cytotoxic T cells, and macrophages destroy infected cells and release antiviral mediators.

Layer 3: Smart Therapeutic Control

Integrated biosensors continuously measure viral load and inflammatory markers. Embedded AI algorithms analyze signals and decide the optimal immune or therapeutic intervention, while microfluidic channels deliver immune modulators and cytokines locally.

📚 Key Clinical & Scientific Foundations

The conceptual BAIL model is anchored in recent clinical trials and preclinical breakthroughs in bioartificial liver support, immunology, and regenerative organ engineering:

HepAssis2® device schematic
CLINICAL TRIAL — PHASE I (2026)

HepAssis2® Bioartificial Liver System

A randomized, open-label phase 1 trial of the HepAssis2® bioartificial liver system in HBV-related acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) patients, a high-endemic-HBV setting. It establishes real clinical demand for bioartificial systems that pair detoxification with active metabolic support — the same rationale behind BAIL's Living Hepatic Tissue Module.

Zhong Z. et al. (2026). Clin Transl Med, 16(2), e70620. doi.org/10.1002/ctm2.70620
UTOpiA tandem circuit
PRECLINICAL BREAKTHROUGH (2025)

The UTOpiA Tandem Circuit (Takebe Group)

A dual-component whole-blood extracorporeal circuit combining an upstream granulocyte/monocyte apheresis column with a downstream bioreactor of HLA-depleted iPSC-derived liver organoids. In rodent ACLF/ALF models, roughly 88% of UTOpiA-treated animals survived versus about 12% of untreated controls — supporting BAIL's tandem immunomodulation-plus-hepatic-support strategy.

Yamaguchi H. et al. (2025). J Hepatol, 84(2), 293–307. doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2025.08.038
Gene-edited porcine liver
FDA-CLEARED TRIAL (2025)

Genetically Engineered Porcine Liver (Extracorporeal Bridging)

The FDA cleared the first clinical trial of a genetically engineered porcine liver (eGenesis' EGEN-5784) used with an extracorporeal liver cross-circulation system for ACLF patients. This establishes a real regulatory pathway for complex, macroscale bioartificial liver-support architectures — precedent relevant to BAIL's translational roadmap.

eGenesis / OrganOx (2025). FDA IND clearance, April 2025. Reported in STAT News and Clinical Trials Arena.
hiHep-BAL device
CLINICAL-GRADE DEVICE (2023–2026)

Clinical-Grade hiHep-BAL Device

A clinical-grade bioartificial liver built with human-induced hepatocytes (hiHeps) manufactured under GMP conditions at scale (~10¹⁰ cells). In an investigator-initiated study of patients after extended liver resection, hiHep-BAL was well tolerated and improved ammonia detoxification and liver regeneration; it is now in a phase 2 trial (NCT05989958) for ACLF — direct evidence that GMP-manufactured human hepatocytes can rescue liver function, as BAIL's Living Hepatic Tissue Module proposes.

Wang Y. et al. (2023). Cell Stem Cell, 30(5). cell.com/cell-stem-cell
Memory T-cell differentiation
IMMUNOLOGY (2022)

Hepatic Immune Surveillance & T-Cell Differentiation in HBV

HBV persistence results from impaired hepatic immune surveillance involving Kupffer cells, NK cells, and virus-specific CD8+ T cells. Restoring these pathways — including durable, self-renewing memory T-cell populations — forms the immunological basis of BAIL's Layer 2 Immune Surveillance Module.

Iannacone M. & Guidotti L.G. (2022). Nat Rev Immunol, 22(1), 19–32. doi.org/10.1038/s41577-021-00549-4

🏛️ Regulatory & Market Landscape

FDA RMAT Pathway

Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy (RMAT) designation supports accelerated development of regenerative, cell-based, and tissue-engineered therapies for serious diseases with unmet medical needs.

EMA ATMP Framework

Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products (ATMPs) provide the EU regulatory pathway for gene therapies, tissue-engineered products, and somatic cell therapies — a foundation for future bioartificial liver technologies.

WHO Hepatitis Elimination Strategy

WHO aims to eliminate viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030, driving demand for innovative curative platforms beyond lifelong antiviral therapy.

Regenerative medicine and cell therapy markets are projected to see sustained double-digit growth through 2035, driven in part by precision HBV therapeutics and next-generation implantable platforms.

🧭 Applications

Chronic HBV Management

Long-term implantable platform for continuous monitoring, immune activation, and functional control in chronic HBV patients.

Personalized Therapeutic Testing

Patient-specific liver model for evaluating antiviral drugs, immunotherapies, and combinatorial treatments with real-time response data.

Liver Regeneration & Failure Support

Supports hepatic tissue regeneration, detoxification, and metabolic functions in acute liver failure and end-stage liver disease.

🚀 Future Directions & Conclusion

Gene Editing Integration

Incorporation of CRISPR/Cas systems for targeted removal of HBV cccDNA and prevention of viral relapse.

Smart Monitoring & AI Optimization

Development of advanced biosensors and AI algorithms for real-time monitoring and adaptive therapeutic control.

Enhanced Tissue Engineering

Optimization of vascularization, scaffold design, and cell crosstalk to improve tissue maturation and long-term implant stability.

Clinical Translation & Scalability

Scaling manufacturing, ensuring long-term safety, and establishing regulatory pathways for clinical implementation.

BAIL combines regenerative medicine, immunotherapy, biosensors, and bioelectronics into a single platform, requiring multidisciplinary GMP manufacturing and long-term clinical validation. Supported by emerging advances in liver organoids, immune microenvironment engineering, and regenerative medicine, this conceptual platform offers a multidisciplinary framework for next-generation precision therapeutics and a potential pathway toward autonomous, patient-specific management of chronic HBV infection.
